How it works

  1. Add your audio. Drop WAV, FLAC, M4A, OGG, AAC or MP3 files, or pick them. Your browser decodes them; formats it cannot read are flagged on their row.
  2. Pick quality and convert. Choose a bitrate, channels and sample rate, then press Convert. Files are encoded to MP3 one at a time in a background worker.
  3. Download. Save each MP3 on its own, or every file at once as a ZIP. Sizes and savings are shown for each file.

Questions, answered

Are my files uploaded anywhere?

No. WAV to MP3 Converter runs entirely in your browser. Your audio is read into memory on your device, decoded by the browser, encoded to MP3 with JavaScript, and never sent to a server. You can disconnect from the internet and it keeps working.

Which bitrate should I choose?

192 kbps is a good default for music. 320 kbps is the highest MP3 quality and makes larger files. For speech, podcasts or phone recordings, 64 or 96 kbps with mono is usually plenty and much smaller.

Why does a FLAC or M4A file show a codec message?

Decoding is done by your browser, and not every browser can read every format. Chrome, Edge and Firefox read FLAC and OGG; Safari reads M4A and AAC well. If a row says the format is not supported, try the same file in another browser.

Is it really free? Is there a size limit?

Yes, it is free and open source under the MIT license, with no account, no daily limits and no ads. Each file can be up to about 500 MB, because decoding expands audio in memory. If it saves you a paid subscription, you can say thanks on Ko-fi.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After your first visit the page is cached by a service worker, so it loads without a connection. You can also install it from your browser's menu as an app.
